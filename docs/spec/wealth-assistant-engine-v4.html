v4 · Lean · Local · Self-Scoring Edition · June 2026

An engine that sees the hidden connections — and grades its own calls every morning.

This revision deliberately makes the engine lighter, more local, and harder to fool. Three changes from the connected edition. (1) The heavy feedback-and-memory loop is gone. Injecting your reactions and a growing case-memory into the model meant constant tuning and gave a non-expert's hunches too much sway over the verdict. In its place: a self-scoring loop — each night the engine simply compares the call it made yesterday with what the market actually did, and keeps a calibration scorecard. No memory to steer, almost nothing to tune. (2) Heavyweight GraphRAG is dropped as too slow, costly, and complex for this stage; the exact, auditable look-through — the part that actually answers "what else of mine holds the thing that's falling?" — stays, and runs entirely local on a Mac mini. (3) A clean two-model split: Claude Code (headless) runs the hard nightly digestion as a small bull/bear agent team; DeepSeek V4 handles the cheap, high-volume chores. The philosophy is unchanged: descriptive factor states, never a fitted composite — the LLM interprets, it does not optimize.

◆ The design principle behind every cut

For an early-stage, single-user system, the cheapest reliable thing beats the cleverest fragile one. So the load-bearing parts are boring and exact — factor states, two-sided dated evidence, point-in-time data, and a deterministic holdings look-through — and the clever parts that needed babysitting (an evolving memory, a GraphRAG pipeline) are removed until they earn their place. The one new mechanism, self-scoring, adds almost no surface to overfit: it is just arithmetic comparing yesterday's stated lean to today's outcome, shown to the digest as a mirror, never as an instruction.

The honest frame is unchanged: signals shorten your lag, not your risk. The engine hands you a sourced, calibrated, conditional lean and a map of what moves with what — each morning. You remain the only thing that clicks buy.

03 / THE FACTOR STATES

Simple, strong, descriptive — never a fitted scoreThe numeric core, kept deliberately un-optimized. Overfitting is a disease of optimization; if you never fit weights to past P&L, there is almost nothing left to overfit.

v4 keeps signal_core's central choice: each raw input becomes a descriptive state — its current reading ranked against its own history and bucketed into a band (extreme_low … extreme_high), carrying a direction, a dated evidence pointer, and a valid flag. There is no weighted composite anywhere. The LLM reads the states and judges, in context and against the news, when each one is economically relevant — it does not compute or optimize them. Three construction rules survive; the factor list is sharpened by the 2026 evidence.

  • Rank against own history, in logic-based bands. Cut-points are constants chosen for economic meaning, never tuned to returns. A failed/stale factor becomes valid:false and the model is told to ignore it — missing ≠ bad, and one broken factor never sinks the snapshot.
  • Read direction, never assume "high = good". In A-shares a stretched up-move is reversal-down risk, not bullish continuation. The state encodes which way "high" points.
  • A handful is enough. The factor-zoo collapses to ~13 themes; the practitioner core is market, value, quality, momentum, low-vol. Naive 1/N beats 14 optimization models out-of-sample (DeMiguel). Add a factor only when it would have changed a past decision you got wrong — not because it is theoretically blessed.

★ Why this beats a fitted dual-momentum composite (the 2026 evidence)

Crowding predicts crashes, not means. Out-of-sample (2001–2024), crowded reversal factors show ~1.7–1.8× higher crash probability, and crowding-based selection earns no alpha — so crowding belongs as a risk gauge, exactly where it sits here. Momentum decayed from ~10%/yr in the 1990s to ~2% and is now crowded with the widest winner-minus-loser dispersion since 1990 — so it stays a gate, never the engine. And short-term reversal — driven by turnover and Amihud illiquidity — is the robust exception that still diversifies, which is why it is the A-share workhorse. The states-not-composite design turns these findings into structure instead of a knob to overfit.

The trend gate is the one hard rule: if a theme is below its 200-day MA, the LLM caps the lean at Hold/Avoid no matter how attractive the story. Never buy a downtrend. Everything else is judgment over states — humbled by a stated base rate, and now by the engine's own recent track record (§06).

Holdings are disclosed on a lag (CN funds quarterly; US 13F quarterly, N-PORT monthly). 05 / THE CONNECTION GRAPH

The overlap you can't see by eye — answered exactly, locallyThis is the feature that motivated the whole upgrade. It is kept, sharpened, and made simple: an exact graph look-up over your holdings, on a durable local graph database. The expensive, fuzzy GraphRAG layer is dropped for this stage.

The killer question — "this name is falling; what else of mine holds it?" — is not a job for a language model. It is set arithmetic over disclosed holdings, and it should be provably correct, auditable, and instant. So the graph layer is one deterministic capability, stored in a real, persistent, on-disk graph database running entirely on your Mac mini.

One falling name (中际旭创) sits inside three funds on your lists at once — invisible by eye, one hop in the graph. A second hop reaches the shared driver, where a QDII you hold is also exposed.

The one capability — deterministic look-through exact · no LLM · local

The graph stores (:Fund)-[:HOLDS {weight, as_of}]->(:Security)-[:EXPOSED_TO]->(:Driver|:Theme), built straight from the §04 holdings feeds — no extraction, no model, no error. "B is falling — who else holds B, and what's my look-through?" is then one query that is fast, exact, and auditable:

// every other item on my lists exposed to a name that fell today, with look-through weight MATCH (s:Security)<-[h:HOLDS]-(f:Fund) WHERE s.ret_1d <= -0.05 AND f.on_my_list = true RETURN s.name, s.ret_1d, collect({fund:f.name, weight:h.weight}) AS exposed_funds, sum(h.weight * f.my_position) AS total_lookthrough ORDER BY total_lookthrough DESC;

It catches the "illusion of diversification" — three funds, one bet — directly, and feeds the connections[] block in the dashboard. It is also the exact tool the emerging-fund screen uses to measure overlap with your existing core (§07), and it makes the crowding state (§03) concrete for your book, not the market's.

⛁ Where it lives — a durable, curated, local graph DB (not an in-memory toy)

The graph is small (a few dozen funds and their holdings), but it must be persistent and dependable, not rebuilt in RAM each run. Use a real embedded/local graph database on the Mac mini: Neo4j Community is the most battle-tested and speaks the Cypher above natively (a local JVM service); KùzuDB's maintained successor LadybugDB is the embedded, single-file, zero-dependency option that needs no server and is built for Apple-Silicon edge use; FalkorDB is a fast, curated choice purpose-built for LLM knowledge graphs. All three persist to disk, keep quarterly snapshots (so the graph is itself point-in-time), and run fully offline. The graph is a curated asset you keep — it is never a throwaway structure.

⊘ Why GraphRAG is dropped (for now)

A full GraphRAG pipeline — LLM-extracted edges, a vector index, community detection, multi-hop retrieval orchestration — is slow, costly, and complex, and for a single-user nightly system it mostly answers questions you are not asking. It is cut. When a genuinely fuzzy, second-order link matters (a shared supplier or end-customer two hops away), the answer is not a new framework: the digest (§08, Claude Code) is simply handed the falling node's one-hop neighbourhood as plain text and asked to reason over it. Same insight, no vector store, no extraction pipeline, no extra infrastructure to keep alive. If the need ever grows, the durable graph already in place is the foundation to add more — but you start exact and cheap.

The schema (FinDKG-inspired, kept deliberately small)

// entity types Fund/ETF · Security/Company · Theme · Driver · Supplier · Region · MacroFactor // relations HOLDS · EXPOSED_TO · SUPPLIES · COMPETES_WITH · DRIVES · LEADS // hard edges (HOLDS, EXPOSED_TO) come straight from holdings feeds — exact, no LLM // every edge carries {as_of, source, valid_from, valid_to} → the graph is TEMPORAL, point-in-time like everything else

Soft, text-derived edges (SUPPLIES, DRIVES, LEADS) are optional and added sparingly, only when a theme needs them; a temporal graph-neural-net for forward link-prediction stays a far-future, human-reviewed idea, not part of the build. The exact look-through is the whole of the graph layer that ships.

06 / SELF-SCORING

It grades itself against reality — not against youThe old feedback-and-memory loop is replaced by one lightweight, objective mechanism: compare last night's call to today's data, and keep score. Almost nothing to tune, and your hunches never steer the verdict.

Two problems with the previous design, both real. Your feedback isn't expert feedback — you said so — and a loop that lets a non-specialist's reactions reshape the model's judgment will quietly teach it your blind spots. And an injected, growing memory is hard to control: it needs constant tuning, can drift, and can carry a wrong lesson forever. So both are cut. What remains is the one signal that is objective and needs no tuning: did the call prove right?

Each dashboard lean already carries a confidence and an invalidation/flip-trigger. That makes it scorable: "Trim, 0.55 confidence, right if it's lower over 10 sessions." No extra habit for you — the engine just logs what it said.

★ Why a proper scoring rule, and why it needs almost no tuning

The Brier score (mean-squared error between stated probability and 0/1 outcome) is a strictly proper rule: the only way to score well is to state honest, calibrated probabilities. Its decomposition separates reliability (are your 70% calls right 70% of the time?) from resolution (do your calls actually separate winners from losers?), and the Brier skill score compares you to simply predicting the base rate — positive means you are adding value, negative means you are not. This directly targets the one documented LLM failure that matters here — overconfidence — and it is just arithmetic over (confidence, outcome) pairs. There are no weights to fit, so there is nothing to overfit.

⊘ The guardrails (kept, but far fewer)

The scorecard is descriptive only. It can nudge the digest to be more or less confident; it can never open the 200-day trend gate (§03), change a factor state, or supply a number. It is shown only after a minimum sample (so noise can't mislead), uses a rolling window (so an old regime can't rule a new one), and lives in the dashboard for you to see. And your own reaction? Demoted to an optional one-tap "useful / not useful" that is logged for your review only — a note to yourself about whether the engine's style is serving you, never a signal fed back into the model.

07 / EMERGING — INDUSTRY → TOP 3 FUNDS

From a faint industry signal to a curated shortlistA two-stage funnel: first qualify the industry, then screen its funds on a fixed scorecard down to a defensible top 3. The mistake to avoid is jumping straight from a hot theme to a ticker.

The redesign separates two questions that v3 blurred: is the theme real and durable? and which fund expresses it best? They need different evidence. Stage A is industry research; Stage B is fund analysis. A theme must clear Stage A before any fund is considered.

Stage A — qualify the industry research, not hype

Start from weak-signal analysis (Ansoff): every big trend begins as faint, ambiguous noise. Sort the text stream into noise → weak → strong and score small-but-accelerating topics — but the new, decisive filter is durability. The 2026 thematic literature is clear that the funds that last share three traits, and the fads that died lacked them.

Stage-A testWhat it asks
1 · Signal strengthAcceleration (most important) + low base + breadth across distinct sources/companies — minus a crowding penalty. Cluster via BERTopic / an LLM tagging pass.
2 · Durability decisiveBroad adoption potential (affects many, not a niche), a credible path to profitability (not just a growth story), and resilience to being a one-cycle fad. AI implementation, cybersecurity, energy transition passed this; SPAC/cannabis/pandemic plays failed it.
3 · Lead-chain corroborationDoes the US relay (§08) confirm real end-demand — capex, orders, contract prices revised up — rather than a domestic narrative alone?
4 · Investable wrapper existsIs there a fund/ETF (or a fund heavily exposed)? If not, it's a watch-only idea — stop here.

Stage B — screen the funds to a top 3 fixed scorecard

Only for a theme that cleared Stage A. Pull the candidate CN funds/ETFs for the theme (AkShare) and score each on the same scorecard every time — the discipline that separates selection from guessing. The output is a ranked top 3 with a one-page comparison the digest can defend.

Promotion still requires the v3 combination: durable theme + corroborated catalyst + not-yet-crowded + low overlap with core.

Lifecycle check that the funnel works: AI-compute optical modules were a textbook Stage-A pass in early 2024 (small base, accelerating 研报 volume, real hyperscaler-capex catalyst, clean ETFs). 08 / THE DECISION LAYER

States → a calibrated lean, argued two waysThe hard nightly reasoning runs in Claude Code, headless, as a small bull/bear agent team — humbled by a base rate and by the engine's own recent scorecard. The cheap chores go to DeepSeek V4.

The literature is blunt: language models are systematically overconfident, more so in finance and outside English — they favour plausible text over honest uncertainty. Three defences stack here. The states-not-composite design is the first (a legible band can't people-please). The two-sided debate is the second. And §06's scorecard is the third — the model is shown, in numbers, where its recent confidence has not been earned.

The macro bridge & lead relay (recap)

Above each theme sits the liquidity layer that moves all risk assets (state 8: Fed path, 10-yr real yield, dollar, USD/CNY) — it rarely triggers a trade; it sets the book-wide risk_dial. Below it runs the universal lead chain, each arrow a lead you can act on before the local names price it: end-demand capex → bellwether vendor guidance → tier-1 supplier guide vs whisper → US-listed proxies → A-share names → local earnings (the laggard). In the graph these are LEADS edges; as a factor it is state 1.

★ Why subagents, not an "Agent Team," and the cost note

In 2026 Claude Code offers both subagents (isolated workers that run and return to the parent) and Agent Teams (teammates that talk to each other mid-task). For a bull/bear read you want the two seats independent — they should not see each other's argument while forming their own — so subagents are the right, stable, cheaper primitive; you'd only reach for an Agent Team if you wanted a live, multi-round rebuttal. Define each seat as a markdown file in .claude/agents/ (frontmatter + system prompt); the orchestrator decides when to spawn them. If both seats share a large common context, running them as forks reuses the parent's prompt cache and is cheaper still. The calibration discipline

  • Consider-the-opposite — the bull and bear seats are structural, not stylistic. Deliberation beats a single prompt for calibration.
  • Base-rate first — state the relevant base rate (champion's curse; what parabolic themes historically give back) before the conviction.
  • Read the scorecard — if §06 shows this state-pattern has been overconfident, the lean's confidence is lowered accordingly.
  • Allocation > timing; sin only a little — default to Hold/low conviction; lean hard only at genuine extremes.
  • Evidence-grounded & auto-downgrade — every field cites a fetched, dated source; conflict/staleness/single-source/forum-only lowers confidence and is logged. No source → omit; never invent a number.
  • Abstain when blind — too many invalid states, or extremes pointing opposite with no resolution → Abstain, not a guess.

Point-in-time, everywhere. Prices, factor states, scored calls, and the holdings graph all carry an as-of stamp and are append-only. 10 / DURABILITY

Keeping it honest for yearsThree things rot a system like this: an overfit factor set, a judgment layer that drifts unchecked, and signals that quietly stop working. v4 removes a whole category of risk by removing the evolving memory.

The design starts with a structural advantage: by emitting descriptive factor states the LLM reads rather than a fitted composite (§03), there is little to overfit — you are reporting whether each factor is high or low against logic-based bands, not tuning weights against past P&L. Overfitting is a disease of optimization; the cure is having little to optimize. The moment you backtest anything — a band, the gate, the rotation — the quant literature's defenses apply, so bake them in.

DisciplineWhat it protects against
Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)Corrects a result's Sharpe for how many variants you tried plus non-normality. A gleaming in-sample Sharpe is a mirage if it's the best of 100 trials.
Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO)Via combinatorially-purged CV: the chance your chosen config ranks below median out-of-sample. High PBO means you fit noise.
Purged / embargoed CV (CPCV)Purges overlapping labels and embargoes a gap so future data can't leak backward. Preferred over plain walk-forward.
Parameter-stability scoreShare of nearby settings (±10–20%) that stay profitable. Above ~70–80% means a robust plateau, not a knife-edge fit.
After-cost & point-in-time, alwaysReport after fees/slippage; reason only on as-of data (§09). The append-only store exists precisely to kill leakage-driven inflation.
★ The self-scoring loop is the durability mechanism for the judgment layer

v3 needed elaborate governance to stop an evolving memory from accumulating errors. v4 removes that whole failure mode by removing the memory. In its place, §06's scorecard does the durability job objectively: it tracks, in numbers, whether the engine's calls are still calibrated and still beating the base rate. If the Brier skill score turns negative, or a state-pattern's hit-rate decays, you see it immediately — that is the early-warning that a once-good signal has crowded out or a regime has shifted. No lesson can compound into rot because no lesson is stored; only outcomes are.

★ The durability rules of thumb

Prefer states to a tuned blend. Prefer robust, well-documented factors — reversal, turnover/illiquidity, the cross-market lead — to exotic ones you found yesterday. Prefer durable, curated infrastructure — a persistent graph DB, an append-only store, maintained data libraries — over quick in-memory hacks that work in a demo and break in a month. And remember factors decay as they crowd: re-check the scorecard quarterly, watch the dispersion, and retire what stops working before it costs you.

13 / LIMITS & DISCIPLINE

What it is, and isn'tRe-read whenever the dashboard sounds certain. The lighter design removes some ways to fool yourself — and leaves the important ones intact.

▲ What the engine gives you

  • A durable process: the same disciplined nightly read on any theme, for years — states and dated evidence, not a vibe.
  • Connection awareness: when a holding falls, an exact local query shows every fund on your lists exposed to it — contagion you'd otherwise miss.
  • Honest confidence: a self-scoring loop that keeps the engine's stated certainty tethered to how its recent calls actually resolved.
  • Less lag: the US→China relay and leading factors buy days-to-weeks over a crowd watching reported earnings.

▼ What it cannot do

  • Predict tops/bottoms. Leads and graphs cut lag and surface links — they don't remove uncertainty.
  • Trust a recalled number. Fetch-don't-recall; the engine reasons only over fetched, dated evidence.
  • Learn finance from you. By design it doesn't — it grades itself against outcomes, not your reactions. That's a feature, given you're not a specialist.
  • Replace your judgment. A tool that agrees faster is more dangerous than none; the scorecard exists to keep it humble.
The shape of using it well

Funds/ETFs only, satellites capped, the boring broad-index core compounding underneath. The connection graph and the self-scoring loop don't change the goal — they sharpen it. The engine's gift was never more trades; it's better-timed, fewer, calmer ones, each with a written reason, each checked against how the last ones actually turned out. Most mornings the right action is none. Patience is a position.
